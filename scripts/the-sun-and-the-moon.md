# The Children Who Became the Sun and the Moon
### 해와 달이 된 오누이 — A SleepyTigerTales bedtime story

**Target runtime:** ~13 minutes
**Narration pace:** ~90 words/min for Act I, slowing to ~70 words/min by the ending.
`(…)` = 2-second pause · `(……)` = 4-second pause · `(breath)` = audible soft inhale, invites the child to breathe along.

---

## Why this story

- One of the two or three most famous Korean folktales — instantly recognizable to
  Korean parents, fresh and exotic to everyone else. Strong search terms in both
  languages.
- It stars a **tiger** — it keeps Horangi, the channel mascot, at the center of a
  second video and starts building a "Horangi folktale universe."
- It is inherently what the channel promises: **beautiful and melancholic**. Two
  children rise into the night sky and become the sun and the moon. It literally
  ends with the moon watching over the listening child — the perfect final image
  for a bedtime video.
- It is an origin story ("why is there a sun and a moon?"), which gives us a
  first-15-seconds hook for free.

**Gentle adaptation notes** (the original is too dark for bedtime):
- Original: the tiger eats the mother. Here: the tiger takes her basket and her
  shawl, and she is lost in the deep woods until the ending.
- Original: the tiger falls to his death; sorghum turns red with blood. Here: he
  tumbles softly into the sorghum field, and the sorghum "still blushes red from
  embarrassment for him."
- Added ending: the mother is lifted into the sky too and becomes the dawn — so the
  separation (the melancholy) resolves into a nightly reunion (the comfort).

## THE SCRIPT

### COLD OPEN — THE HOOK (0:00–0:25)

> **[VISUAL: The most beautiful shot of the video. A child's window at night, seen
> from inside a warm room. A huge, soft full moon fills the frame. Slow, almost
> imperceptible push-in.]**

Have you ever wondered, little one… (…) why the moon comes to your window every
single night… and watches over you while you sleep? (……)

Tonight, I'll tell you the secret. (…) Because a long, long time ago… there was no
moon in the sky at all. (…) And no sun, either. (breath)

There were only two children… a brother and a sister… who lived at the edge of a
deep, dark forest in old Korea. (……) This is their story.

### ACT I — THE MOTHER AND THE MOUNTAIN (0:25–3:30)
*Pace: normal-slow. The story engine — the tiger — arrives before the one-minute mark.*

> **[VISUAL: A small thatched-roof house at the foot of misty blue mountains. Dusk.
> A mother in a pale hanbok ties a cloth bundle of rice cakes to her back.]**

In that little house lived a gentle mother, and her two children — a brave older
brother named **Haneul**, and his little sister, **Dal-rae**. (…)

Every day, the mother crossed the tall mountain to sell her rice cakes in the
village on the other side. And every evening she crossed back home, when the sky
turned the color of ripe persimmons. (……)

But on this evening, little one… someone was waiting on the mountain path. (…)

> **[VISUAL: A winding mountain pass in deep violet twilight. Horangi the tiger —
> same design as always: orange, black stripes, white belly, golden eyes — sits in
> the middle of the path. He is big, but his face is more hungry than mean.]**

A tiger. (…) A very big… very hungry tiger. (breath)

And the tiger said, in a voice as low as thunder rolling far away:

**"Grandmother, grandmother… give me a rice cake… and I won't gobble you up."** (……)

The mother's heart went *thump, thump, thump*. She quickly gave him a rice cake,
and the tiger let her pass. (…)

But at the next bend in the path… there he was again. (…)

**"Give me a rice cake… and I won't gobble you up."** (……)

So she gave him another. (…) And at the next bend… (…) and the next… (…) and the
next… the same low voice, each time a little slower, a little sleepier…

**"Give me a rice cake… and I won't… gobble you up."** (……)

> **[VISUAL: The mother's basket, empty. A single cloth ribbon flutters. Horangi
> holds her shawl in his paws, looking pleased with himself.]**

Until at last… the basket was empty. (…) Every last rice cake was gone. (breath)

And the greedy tiger took her basket, and her warm shawl too, and the poor mother
hurried away into the deep, dark woods, so far from the path that she could not
find her way home. (……)

The tiger wrapped the shawl around his big striped shoulders, and he padded down
the mountain… toward the little house… where the two children waited alone. (……)

### ACT II — THE PAW AT THE DOOR (3:30–6:30)
*Pace: slow. This is the famous suspense scene — keep it hushed, never loud.*

> **[VISUAL: The house at night. Warm lantern light glows through the paper door.
> Inside, Haneul and Dal-rae sit under one blanket.]**

*Knock… knock.* (…)

"Children… it's your mother… open the door." (……)

But Haneul was a clever boy. He tilted his head. (…) "That doesn't sound like our
mother," he whispered. "Our mother's voice is soft, like falling snow." (…)

"I… caught a cold on the mountain," said the voice, coughing. (……)

"Then show us your hand," said little Dal-rae, "through the hole in the door." (…)

> **[VISUAL: Extreme close-up: a big orange paw pokes gently through the paper
> door. The children's wide eyes in the lantern light.]**

And through the paper door came… (…) a great… big… furry… orange paw. (……)

"Mother's hands are not furry!" whispered Haneul. (breath) And quiet as two little
mice, the children slipped out the back door… ran to the old well in the yard…
and climbed high, high up into the tall willow tree beside it. (……)

> **[VISUAL: The tiger peers into the round dark well. Two small faces shimmer in
> the water — the children's reflection.]**

The tiger searched here… and there… and then he looked into the well. And what did
he see, little one? (…) Two children, deep in the water! (…)

He reached in with his paw — *splash* — but he caught only ripples. (…) He tried to
scoop them out with the gourd dipper — *splish* — but he caught only moonlight. (……)

And up in the tree, little Dal-rae… couldn't help it… she giggled. (…)

The tiger looked up. (……) "Ahh," he purred. "There you are." (breath)

"How did you climb so high, little ones? Tell your friend the tiger."

And Haneul, clever Haneul, called down: "We rubbed the tree with sesame oil! Try
it!" (…)

So the tiger rubbed the tree with sesame oil, and he climbed — and *whoops* — down
he slid, round and round, like a big stripy acorn. (…)

But then sweet, sleepy Dal-rae — who was getting very tired — yawned and said:
"Silly tiger… you should use the axe to make little steps…" (……)

"Dal-rae!" whispered Haneul. (…) But it was too late. *Chop… chop… chop…* the tiger
began to climb. (……)

### ACT III — THE ROPE FROM HEAVEN (6:30–9:30)
*Pace: slower still. Longer pauses. This is the emotional summit — awe, not fear.*

> **[VISUAL: The children at the very top of the tree, stars enormous above them.
> The whole world — mountains, house, well — tiny and blue below.]**

The children climbed to the very top of the tree, where the branches were thin and
the stars felt close enough to touch. (……) There was nowhere left to go… except
up. (breath)

So Haneul held his sister's hand, and they looked at the wide, dark sky, and they
said, very softly:

**"Sky, dear sky… if you love us… please send down a strong rope."** (……)

And little one… (…) the sky was listening. (……)

> **[VISUAL: A shimmering golden rope unspools slowly from a part in the clouds,
> glowing like the first ray of dawn. The children take hold. They rise — slowly,
> gently, like leaves floating upward.]**

Down from the clouds came a rope of gold, strong and warm, and the moment the
children held on… it lifted them, ever so gently… up… (…) and up… (…) and up…
(……) past the owl's nest… past the mountain top… past the highest, quietest
clouds. (……)

Far below, the tiger saw it all. And he called out too: "Sky! Send ME a rope!" (…)

And the sky sent him one. (…) But the sky knows the hearts of tigers who take rice
cakes and shawls that are not theirs… and this rope was old, and frayed, and soft
as an overcooked noodle. (…)

The tiger climbed… and climbed… and — *snap!* — (…) down he tumbled, head over
tail, over head, over tail… and landed — *whumph!* — deep in a big, soft field of
sorghum. (……)

He was not hurt. (…) But oh, was he embarrassed. (…) And to this very day, little
one, the sorghum plants of Korea blush **red**… remembering the night a tiger fell
into their field. (……)

And the tiger? He padded back up his mountain, quiet and small-feeling, and he
never, ever pretended to be somebody's mother again. (breath)

### ACT IV — THE FIRST MOONRISE (9:30–12:00)
*Pace: very slow now. This is the wind-down. Each sentence softer than the last.*

> **[VISUAL: Above the clouds. A calm ocean of silver mist. The children stand on
> the clouds as if on soft snow.]**

High above the clouds, the sky spoke to the children in a voice like wind through
pine trees. (……)

"Dear children… the world below is dark. It has no light at all. (…) Will you stay
with me… and become its light?" (……)

Haneul looked at his little sister. Dal-rae looked at her big brother. (…) And
they nodded. (breath)

"Then you, Haneul, shall be the **sun**," said the sky. (…)

But little Dal-rae's lip trembled. "Please…" she whispered. "I'm afraid of the
dark. I don't want to be alone at night." (……)

And her brother — because that is what big brothers do — said, "Then take the day,
Dal-rae. (…) Take the warm, bright day. (…) I will take the night… and I will
watch over everyone who sleeps." (……)

> **[VISUAL: Dal-rae begins to glow gold and rises east; Haneul glows silver-white
> and drifts west. Between them, the first dawn breaks over old Korea.]**

And so the sister became the **sun**… warm and golden, so bright that no one can
stare at her — because even the sun can be a little shy. (……)

And the brother became the **moon**. (……) Quiet… (…) and silver… (…) and patient…
keeping watch over every sleeping village… every sleeping forest… (…) every
sleeping child. (……)

And the mother? (…) The sky had not forgotten her, lost in the deep woods,
calling for her children. (…) It lifted her up too, ever so gently… and she became
the **dawn** — that soft pink light between the night and the morning. (……)

So now, every single morning, the sun-sister rises into her mother's arms. (…)
And every single evening, the moon-brother sinks back into them. (……) They were
never really apart again. (…) Not even for one day. (breath)

### CLOSING LULLABY (12:00–13:00)
*Pace: slowest. Half-whisper. Long pauses. The child should be asleep by now; this
section is for the ones still blinking.*

> **[VISUAL: Return to the opening shot — the child's window, the full moon. The
> moon now has the faintest suggestion of a boy's gentle face. Snow begins to fall,
> very slowly. Fade over sixty seconds.]**

So tonight, little one… when the moon looks in your window… (……) you'll know who
he is. (……)

He is the brother who gave away the day… (…) so that no child would ever be alone
in the dark. (……)

*Jajang… jajang…* sleep now, little one. (……)

The sun is resting. (……)

The dawn is waiting. (……)

And the moon… (…) is watching over you. (……)

Goodnight. (……)

*(Music only — soft gayageum and slow breathing-tempo pads — for a final 30–60
seconds before fade to black. No end-card voice-over.)*

## Production continuity sheet (fixes the #1 visual problem in the last video)

Every image/video generation prompt must include this block verbatim:

> *Traditional Joseon-era Korea, pre-modern: thatched-roof hanok houses, paper
> doors, stone walls, pine forests, no vehicles, no power lines, no modern
> buildings or clothing. Night scenes: deep blue-violet palette, warm lantern
> gold accents, soft film grain, gentle mist. Style: soft painterly 2D
> storybook illustration, muted colors, consistent across all shots.*

**Character sheet (restate in every prompt):**
- **Horangi (tiger):** orange with black stripes, white belly and muzzle, golden
  eyes, no clothing ever, expressive eyebrows, more hungry/foolish than scary.
- **Haneul (brother, ~8):** short black hair, white jeogori top, gray pants.
- **Dal-rae (sister, ~5):** black hair in two small buns, pale-pink hanbok.
- **Mother:** hair in a low bun with a wooden pin (binyeo), dove-gray hanbok,
  cloth bundle on her back.

**Time-of-day map (one per act, never mixed):** Act I dusk → Acts II–III night →
Act IV celestial/dawn → Closing night. If a shot's lighting doesn't match its
act, regenerate it.

**Audio:** single warm narrator, close-mic, generous room reverb; music at −20 dB
under narration; tempo ≤ 60 bpm throughout, slowing further in Act IV.
